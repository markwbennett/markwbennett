  Alternates: *It is well settled. That does not make it correct.* /

## 4. Tests that emerged, and that killed most candidates

1. **The phone test.** Someone hears the name spoken and must type it. Kills
   Hephaestus; taxes quaere, inventio, subsilentio. **Note:** this test loses most
   of its force for a sub-brand or a publication, since nobody types those from
   hearing — it governs only the thing on the door.
2. **Whose act does the word name?** *Sub silentio* and *per incuriam* name the
   **court's** failure; *unbriefed* / *unargued* name the **bar's**. Names built
   on someone's omission put blame in the brand. Advocate-side words — inventio,
   assay, question presented, quaere (the critical reader's note) — do not.
3. **Rank claims are the one real rules exposure.** Texas Rule 7.02(a)(4) bars
   comparing your services with other lawyers' unless substantiated by
   verifiable, objective data. *Visionary* asserts a quality; *Leading* asserts a
   rank, which is the exact trigger. Rule 7.01 (rewritten in the amendments
   approved July 1, 2021, now "Communications Concerning a Lawyer's Services")
   permits a trade name that is not false or misleading. **Never claim what you
   can prove**: board certification and the CCA record are substantiable;
   adjectives are not.
4. **The place-noun is a crutch for a vague root.** *Freedom* needs "labs";
   *question presented* does not.
5. **Manufacture vs discovery.** Foundry / forge / smithy / mill promise
   *well-made*. Lab / studio / observatory / station promise *new*. The positioning
   wants the second family.
6. **Name the finding, not the flair.** Imagination / creativity / creation name
   the lawyer's mental flair, which is what an opponent attacks.

## 6. Retired, and why

- `firstimpression.law` — wrong theory. These are not first-impression questions.
- `unsettled.law` — asserts the question is **open**. It is closed, and closed
  wrong.
- `subsilentio`, `arguendo`, `per incuriam` — the court's act, not mine.
- Visionary, Leading, Best — rank/quality claims; 7.02(a)(4) exposure.
  ("Visionaries" is also Gartner's lower-right quadrant: vision without execution.)
- Foundry, Forge, Smithy, Lab, Studio, Atelier, Institute, Studies, Study Group,
  Launchpad, Hephaestus, Daedalus — occupied, misleading as to status, selling the
  wrong virtue, or unspellable. Detail in `appellate-names.md`.
- Occupied, confirmed: `thefoundry.law`, `foundrylawgroup.com`, `foundry.legal`,
  `briefwright.com` (Oregon criminal-appellate lawyer), `writ.works`,

## 7. Resolved by the manifesto (Making Law: Solving Old Problems, Aug. 3, 2026)

The paper answers most of what was open. Key points, in my own words from it:

- **Opinion laundering, defined:** a harmful proposition appears in one early case
  — the **root case**. The next generation cites the root; the generation after
  cites those. By the fourth generation forty-two cases appear to stand for the
  proposition with extensive cross-support, and every new lawyer concludes the law
  is settled. "The question is analyzed once, if at all, but with generations of
  interlocking citations... the courts make it appear that the opinion has more
  support than that one case." The root may have held something narrower, or been
  about a different question, or been decided when everything was different.
- **The cure is mine already:** "reversing the opinion-laundering process. Trace
  the proposition back to its root. Read what the root case actually said. Figure
  out why it doesn't apply to this situation, or why it was wrong then, or why
  it's wrong now." I call the omission a failure to do "this archaeology."
- **"Settled" means:** the CCA has rejected *this specific argument*, on facts
  like these, after full briefing on the point.
- **The intro line:** "The rule that has stood unchallenged for fifty years has
  not been confirmed by fifty years of examination; it has been laundered by fifty
  years of repetition."
- **Existing institutional name:** *Institute for Advanced Criminal Law Studies*,
  Houston — already in use as the byline for the scholarship.
- **The strategy:** take your own idea seriously; find the path (years-long — Owens
  came a dozen years after the 42.07 litigation began); write for textualist and
  originalist judges. *Navarro* (Yeary, J., concurring, joined by Schenck, P.J.):
  "less stare decisis and more recte decide."
