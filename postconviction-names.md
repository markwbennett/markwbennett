# Narrowed: a cutting-edge artisanal post-conviction practice

## What "post-conviction" changes about the vocabulary

Trial names sell doubt. Post-conviction names sell *correction of a finished
record* — so the live words are writ, habeas, coram nobis, vacatur, errata, the
cold record, collateral review, newly discovered evidence.

## What "artisanal" changes about the suffix

Drop `labs` and `stack`. The maker suffixes — **wright, smith, forge, foundry,
bench, works, press** — carry small-batch craft, which is what a post-conviction
practice actually is: a handful of records, worked slowly, by hand. "Cutting
edge" then comes from the *root* word being precise and unfamiliar, not from the
suffix being a startup cliché.

There's also a rules reason to drop `labs` here specifically: for a
post-conviction practice, "Labs" invites the reader to think forensic
laboratory — DNA, toxicology, a testing capability. Under the not-misleading
half of Texas Rule 7.01 that's the one suffix in the original list with real
exposure. (See the caveat in `names.md`; confirm the current rule text.)

## The narrowed list

Ranked. Everything here screened clear of a commercial occupant unless noted.

### 1. writwright.com

A *wright* is one who makes: wheelwright, shipwright, playwright. You make
writs. It is a real English word-formation, not a coinage, which is why it reads
artisanal instead of cute — and it is precisely, only, post-conviction work.
Screen: nothing found. A differently-spelled "WriteWright" media page exists on
Facebook; different string, different field.

### 2. vacatur.co (or vacaturworks.com)

*Vacatur* is the deliverable — the judgment set aside. A single abstract Latin
noun is exactly the current register for a serious modern firm, and it promises
the remedy without promising the result. Screen: only law-review and
administrative-law usage; no company found. Nearest commercial neighbors are
unrelated vacation-rental brands (Vacasa, Vacatia).

### 3. coldrecord.com (or thecoldrecord.com)

The cold record is your raw material: the thing everyone else has stopped
reading. Evocative, plain-English, and it says "I work from the paper" without
saying it. Screen: "The Cold Record" is a touring theater piece by Rude Mechs —
a show, not a domain occupant — plus several unrelated music labels using
"Cold Record(ings)."

### 4. writsmith.com

Same logic as writwright, blunter and more guild-like. Screen: the only hit is a
niche community term on Pluralpedia; no company.

### 5. writfoundry.com

"Micro-foundry" is the literal artisanal-tech frame. Screen: no occupant;
nearest neighbor is Writ Large, an LA font micro-foundry — different name, and
arguably a compliment to the register you're aiming at.

### 6. coramlabs.com / coramworks.com / coramnobis.co

*Coram nobis* translates roughly as "the errors before us," which is the whole
job description. Screen: no firm found under Coram Labs. Caution: "Coram" alone
is carried by unrelated charities and healthcare companies, so the bare word is
probably gone; the compound is likelier to be free. Prefer `coramworks` over
`coramlabs` per the forensic-lab point above.

### 7. reversibleerror.com

The thing you hunt for, named. More insider than the others and a little
defeatist-sounding to a layperson ("error" front and center), but unmistakable
to judges and lawyers. Screen: only dictionary and treatise usage; no practice
found.

### 8. erratalabs.com / errata.works

*Errata* is the corrections pass on something already published — a genuinely
good metaphor for collateral review, and the most "cutting-edge" sounding option
here. Two marks against it: the lab problem above, and Errata Security
(erratasec.com), an established Atlanta infosec firm founded in 2006, already
owns the errata-in-tech association. Use `errata.works` if you use it at all.

## Cut from the earlier list, for this brief

- **writ.works** — occupied. It is the personal site of a writer/facilitator.
- **habeaslabs / habeasworks** — the root is now a legaltech vendor's brand
  (habeas.ai, Australian legal-research AI). Post-conviction is exactly the
  field where you'd be confused with them.
- **elevenoseven.com** (for art. 11.07) — the insider signal is lovely, but at
  least two companies trade as "Eleven O Seven" (an IT shop and a hat maker), so
  the good spellings are likely gone.
- **exculpo, acquitlabs, reasonabledoubtlabs, voirdirelabs** — all still fine
  names, but they're trial names. Doubt and voir dire are not what you sell after
  the verdict.
- **sixthlabs, blackletterlabs** — occupied, per `names.md`.

## Pick, if you want one

**writwright.com**, with **vacatur.co** as the fallback if you'd rather the name
state the remedy than the craft. Writwright is the only candidate that gets
artisanal and post-conviction into one word without a suffix doing the work.
